Educational Disparities Across India

Analyzing UDISE+ and ASER Survey Data to Uncover Inequalities in Access and Outcomes

Project Overview

This project examines educational disparities across India using data from UDISE+ (Unified District Information System for Education Plus) and ASER Survey. Through a series of data visualizations, I explore patterns of inequality across geographic, gender, and socioeconomic dimensions to understand the challenges facing India's education system.

Key Questions: How do enrollment rates vary across Indian states? What gender disparities exist in education access? How do rural and urban areas compare in educational outcomes?

I. Access & Enrollment Disparities

Gender Gap in School Enrollment

This diverging bar chart shows gender disparities in enrollment across states. Northern states like Haryana and Punjab exhibit substantial gaps favoring boys, while southern states like Kerala and Tamil Nadu approach gender parity. The gender gap has reduced notably between 2022-2024.

Educational Progression and Dropout Patterns

This visualization examines enrollment patterns by gender and state. Enrollment drops dramatically between Class 10 and Class 11 nationwide, with similar patterns for both genders.

School Type Distribution: Rural vs Urban Divide

Rural areas remain heavily dominated by state government schools, while urban areas show increasing privatization - urban private unaided schools grew between 2021-2024. Rural private school enrollment has remained stable.

II. Educational Inputs: School Infrastructure

This map shows the composite infrastructure index (electricity, internet, libraries, girls' toilets) across states for 2022-2024. Southern and western states score above 80%, while northeastern states lag below 50%, revealing systematic regional disparities.

III. Learning Outcomes: Measuring Educational Success

This dual heatmap displays reading and math proficiency across Standards III, V, and VIII for Indian states, using ASER 2018, 2022, and 2024 data. Northern and northeastern states achieve highest outcomes (80-90% reading), while southern states surprisingly lag despite superior infrastructure. Mathematics proficiency remains significantly lower across all states.

State Similarity Network: Uncovering Education System Archetypes

This network uses hierarchical clustering on five education metrics—reading proficiency, math proficiency, infrastructure quality, student-teacher ratio, and rural school percentage—to identify states with similar education profiles. Edge thickness represents similarity strength, with connections appearing only when states exceed 70% similarity across all dimensions. The clustering reveals a striking infrastructure paradox. Southern and western states (Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Telangana) form a distinct cluster with India's best school infrastructure yet achieve the poorest reading outcomes. Meanwhile, states like Himachal Pradesh and Mizoram cluster together based on educational success despite moderate resources, while Bihar, Jharkhand, and Uttar Pradesh outperform well-resourced states in mathematics despite extreme overcrowding (high student-teacher ratio).

Data & Methods

The data used for this project comes from 3 sources:

1) UDISE+ (Unified District Information System for Education Plus): This comprehensive database provides school-level information across India, including enrollment statistics by gender and location, infrastructure metrics (electricity, internet, libraries, toilets), and student-teacher ratios. The data covers the period 2021-2024 and represents nearly all schools in India. Available at: UDISE+ Dashboard

2) ASER (Annual Status of Education Report) 2024: Conducted by Pratham, ASER is India's largest citizen-led survey measuring children's learning outcomes. It assesses basic reading (ability to read Standard II level text) and arithmetic (ability to perform division) skills across Standards III, V, and VIII in India. Available at: ASER Centre

Data Processing: All cleaning, merging, and analysis performed in Python (pandas, NumPy). Visualizations created with Altair/Vega-Lite. Full code and data available at GitHub.

Analytical Challenges

Data integration: Combining UDISE+ and ASER data required aligning state names, time periods, and measurement units. ASER's incomplete public data necessitated manual extraction from PDF reports.

Composite indices: Creating the infrastructure index required normalizing and weighting four metrics (electricity, internet, libraries, toilets) into a single comparable measure.

State clustering: Hierarchical clustering on five metrics required normalizing different scales and determining optimal cluster numbers to reveal distinct state profiles.

Final Thoughts

This analysis reveals that educational disparities in India are driven more by teaching quality than infrastructure or class size. Resource-rich southern states fail to convert investments into learning outcomes, while states like Himachal Pradesh and Mizoram achieve excellence with moderate resources.

Three critical challenges emerge: the dramatic Class 10-11 dropout crisis, persistent gender gaps (up to 7 percentage points in northern states), and a mathematics proficiency crisis where fewer than 40% of Standard VIII students can perform division.

Addressing these disparities requires focusing on classroom pedagogy, teacher training, and curriculum effectiveness rather than purely traditional infrastructure investment. States should adopt targeted interventions based on their specific challenges rather than generic national policies.
